# 11 · Carrier PCB (v2, 100 × 100 mm, 2-layer)

**Scope:** the board behind the Pi in the [printed enclosure](10-enclosure-and-thermal.md). It carries:
- the ESP32 DevKit and the CC1101 module on sockets;
- the 12 V protection;
- two TPS54560B bucks: 5.17 V / 5 A for the Pi and the TS-7, 5.17 V / 1 A for the ESP32 side;
- the INA226 power monitors, the blinker optos, the output drivers and the connectors.

Order notes, hand assembly, bring-up and rework are in the [carrier README](../01-hardware/v2/carrier/README.md).

**Maturity:** generated and checked, not yet built:
- the schematic and layout come from [carrier_design.py](../01-hardware/v2/carrier/carrier_design.py) and [carrier_layout.py](../01-hardware/v2/carrier/carrier_layout.py), via [build_carrier.py](../01-hardware/v2/carrier/build_carrier.py) (KiCad 10 + Freerouting);
- the Gerbers, JLCPCB BOM and CPL are in [03-manufacturing/v2_carrier](../03-manufacturing/v2_carrier/).

**Verdict: Watch, ready to order.** The board passes all its automated checks:
- ERC and DRC with zero errors, zero warnings, nothing unconnected and an exact schematic match (parity);
- the netlist matches the firmware's pin map;
- the high-current paths are wide copper laid by design rather than left to the autorouter;
- JLCPCB's own footprints confirm the CPL rotations.

What's left is what only a built board can show: heat at 5 A, AM-band noise, the radio noise floor, and the fit of your exact DevKit. Each has a rework path on the board.

## Premortem: the carrier board failed. Why?

| # | How it fails | Likely | Impact | Prevention / detection | Status |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| PCB-P1 | A net is swapped between the spec and the board | Low | Respin | Scripted schematic; netlist checked against `pins_v2.h` (R10); DRC parity | **Fixed** |
| PCB-P2 | A footprint is wrong: the parts don't fit, or JLCPCB places a diode backwards | Medium | Respin or rework | Standard KiCad footprints; the three custom ones (DevKit sockets, CC1101 socket, the LTV-357T land pattern that the ICPL-356-50C fits) checked against the datasheets and JLCPCB's footprints; CPL fitted to JLCPCB's pads by pin name. Check the polarized parts in JLCPCB's preview, and print the 1:1 assembly drawing and lay the sockets and terminals on it | Fixed (design) + You (preview, 1:1) |
| PCB-P3 | Cold joints on the hand-soldered parts (5 THT connectors + 10 chunky SMD power parts) fail after months of vibration | Low | Intermittent faults | Thermal reliefs on every through-hole pour connection and on `C104`'s GND pad (`PCB-21`); the other 9 SMD power pads sit on small local pours; lead-free HASL; flux and a 60–80 W chisel tip; a dab of RTV on the sockets | Fixed (design) + You |
| PCB-P4 | 5 A overheats the copper | Low | Brown-outs | 2 mm straps on 1 oz copper, plus stitched power pours on L1 and L2 | **Fixed** |
| PCB-P5 | The Pi buck or the diode runs hot in a closed box in summer | Medium | Shutdown, then the Pi resets | ~1.7 W at 3 A; the diode and the IC sit on GND and switch-node copper with 4 tented 0.60/0.30 mm PowerPAD thermal vias into the L2 GND pour. ## Before you move on

- Order: the [carrier README](../01-hardware/v2/carrier/README.md#order-it-at-jlcpcb) walks through the JLCPCB form. The quote must read 100 × 100 mm, 2 layers, and merchandise < $59.00 so Global Standard Direct Line unlocks; check the polarized parts in the placement preview.
- When the boards arrive: hand-solder the 10 chunky SMD power parts first (from DigiKey or LCSC), then the 5 THT connectors (sockets, screw terminals and fan header), then run the bring-up steps before anything goes near the car.
- Bench tests B4 (Pi 5 V under load), B5 (AM radio) and B6 (radio noise floor) now run on the real board.
