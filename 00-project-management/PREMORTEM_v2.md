# Premortem v2: blind-spot cameras + TPMS, and the 100 × 100 mm carrier PCB

**Date:** 2026-11-25. **Scope:** everything added for v2 — Raspberry Pi 4B, your SunFounder TS-7 7" HDMI touch display, two mirror cameras, blinker sensing, amber blind-spot LEDs, a buzzer, and the 6-layer ESP32 carrier PCB — and whether that PCB can be ordered. The v1 TPMS panel keeps its own premortem in [PREMORTEM.md](PREMORTEM.md); its items still apply to the TPMS part.

## Short answer

**Did the earlier v2 premortem cover everything? No.** It had 12 items, and five of its "fixes" were wrong themselves (a CAN pin that breaks flashing, a power latch that was never designed, LEDs placed in the airbag path, a Pi hole pattern that lands under the ESP32). This pass found 40+ more risks. Everything that can be fixed on paper is now fixed and checked by tests or scripts. What is left needs your hands in the car or on the bench.

**Your two additions are in.** A printable enclosure holds the display, the Pi and the PCB as one unit: [01-hardware/enclosure](../01-hardware/enclosure/README.md), generated, with 63 automated fit checks. The touch screen replaces the v1 rotary encoder, menu buttons, OLED and tyre LEDs, and the mute button too. The 12 V piezo sits inside the unit, behind the bezel's right intake slots, and every beep stops by itself: a blind-spot warning after 4 s while the door LED keeps flashing, a TPMS warning after two reminders. Both car USB passthroughs from v1 move into one printed twin wing on the unit's right edge, in v1's Ø25.8 × 23.8 mm double-D cuts.

**OBD-II or tapping the indicators? Tap the indicators.** Standard OBD-II has no turn-signal data. On this car the blinker exists only as a Mazda-private CAN broadcast, which a Bluetooth ELM327 can't follow reliably, and a dropped Bluetooth link would look like "not signalling": no warning, silently. Your dongle is still useful: the car's own speed (PID 0x0D) is now the preferred input of the speed gate, with the GPS as the fallback. No PCB change. Details: [OBD-II dongle](#obd-ii-dongle-optional-speed-source).

**Redrawn for your display, the SunFounder TS-7.** The first enclosure was drawn for the Official 7" Touch Display (DSI ribbon, mounted upside down). The TS-7 is an HDMI display with USB touch: a 165 × 124 mm board with four mounting ears, 7.75 mm thick, its HDMI and micro-USB sockets on the back at the right edge. Now:
- it hangs in the bezel by its ears, the normal way up (IPS);
- video goes over a 20 cm HDMI flat-cable kit;
- power and touch share one split micro-USB lead: 5 V from the carrier's J5, data to a Pi USB port.

The unit stands 21.8 mm proud of the trim (13 mm before) and needs 56 mm of pocket depth (66 before). SunFounder publishes no drawing and its listed height (121 mm) disagrees with its photos (~124.4 mm), so measure the display before printing: [enclosure README, Measure first](../01-hardware/enclosure/README.md#measure-first-then---set).

## Ranked failure stories

Imagine it is a year from now and the system is out of the car. These are the likely reasons, grouped by area, most likely first inside each group. *Status:* **Fixed (design)** = in the spec, pin map or template. **Fixed (firmware)** = in `02-firmware/v2` and unit-tested. **You** = needs a measurement or bench test. **Phase 2** = deliberately later.

## The v2 design after this pass

**Enclosure:** [make_enclosure.py](../01-hardware/enclosure/make_enclosure.py) builds, for your SunFounder TS-7:

- a bezel the display hangs in by its four ears, with intake slots beside the screen; the screen sits 8 mm toward the driver so the HDMI and micro-USB plugs fit in the bezel's right-hand side;
- a housing: a flat plate with three intake windows and the display-cable slot, and the rear box for the Pi and the PCB;
- a lid with the fan;
- a twin USB wing on the bezel's right edge for both car USB passthroughs (from v1, in 2× Ø25.8 × 23.8 mm double-D cuts, 39 mm pitch); the 12 V piezo sits inside, behind the bezel's right intake slots.

Overall 205.6 × 129.0 × 70.25 mm, mounted the normal way up (IPS), 21.8 mm proud of the trim. The rear box (112 × 124 mm, 50.5 mm behind the plate) drops into the console pocket, or the AMPS pattern takes a RAM base on the dash. The PCB sits behind the Pi, parts facing the lid, inside these keep-outs: 16 mm part height, a fan zone, the SMA strip, corners free of pins.

**Display:** video from the Pi's HDMI0 over a straight micro-HDMI adapter, a 20 cm HDMI flat cable and a straight HDMI-A adapter in the TS-7; power and touch on one split micro-USB lead: 5 V + GND from J5 pair 2, D+ / D− + GND to a Pi USB port (red wire cut on that leg), so the Pi's USB ports don't power the display.

**Controls:** the touch screen for everything, mute included; no physical button. The 12 V piezo (IO13) sits inside the dash unit on J4 (4-pin JST-GH; its button pins, IO4, stay free). No encoder, menu buttons, OLED or tyre LEDs.

**Blinker and speed:** the blinker comes from the two opto taps on the 12 V lamp feeds (G9), never from OBD-II. The speed gate takes the car's own speed from your OBD-II dongle while it answers (PID 0x0D: ESP32 Bluetooth Classic → ELM327, 5 Hz), else the GPS, else the 10 s hold, then LEDs only. The `$ESP` line tells the Pi which source is in use (`O` / `G` / `N`).

**Power (P1, ACC-only):** one Add-A-Circuit (5 A) on a non-critical ACC fuse → J1 (5.08 mm screw terminal: +12 V, GND, AUX sense in and its return) → 4 A NANO2 fuse → SMCJ24CA → SUD50P06-15 P-MOSFET reverse protection → π filter (6.8 µH; 10 µF in, 2 × 10 µF + 47 µF/50 V out) → `12V_PROT` → 10 mΩ shunt (INA226, 0x40) → `12V_BUS`. All of it is on the [carrier](../01-hardware/v2/carrier/README.md).

- `12V_BUS` → TPS54560B (441 kHz) → 5.17 V `5V_PI` → 10 mΩ shunt (INA226, 0x41) → J5 (5.08 mm screw terminal, 3 × 5 V / GND): the 90° USB-C pigtail to the Pi, 5 V/GND to the TS-7's split micro-USB lead, one spare pair. EN: 1 MΩ / 150 kΩ (on at 8.0 V) + 10 µF (1.2–1.4 s start delay), a 2N7002 through 100 Ω pulls it low on `PI_INHIBIT`; a 22 k / 33 k rail-up divider → GPIO15.
- `12V_BUS` → TPS54560B (485 kHz) → 5.17 V `5V_ESP` (DevKit VIN through an SS14, LEDs, GPS, the lid fan header) → XC6206 → `3V3` (I2C parts) → ferrite → `3V3_RF` (CC1101).
- `12V_BUS` (after the 10 mΩ shunt, so the INA226 sees a shorted piezo lead) → 0.5 A PTC → J4 BUZ → 12 V active piezo (3–24 V type) inside the dash unit, 2N7002 low side (60 V) on GPIO13 with a flyback diode; J4's button pins (GPIO4, the v1 RC) stay free.
- ACC sense: 100 kΩ / 10 kΩ + 100 nF + clamp diode to 3V3, 1 kΩ → GPIO32.

**Pins:** [pins_v2.h](../02-firmware/v2/include/pins_v2.h). The CC1101, I2C and buzzer pins are those of v1, so the v1 radio driver and EG53MA4 decoder are reused unchanged. All 25 DevKit GPIOs are used; GPIO12 stays unconnected.

**Pi side:** Bookworm 64-bit Lite, overlay file system on, `enable_uart=1`, `dtoverlay=disable-bt`, no serial console, `dtparam=watchdog=on`, the app as a systemd service with `Restart=always`, journal in RAM.

**Phase 2 (no PCB change):** a CANable-type USB CAN adapter on the Pi, listen-only on HS-CAN (OBD pins 6/14): wheel speeds, gear, and the blinker broadcast as a cross-check of the lamp taps (opendbc's 2017 Mazda IDs, to confirm on this car, C20). The Infineon 60 GHz radar on Pi GPIOs.

## What to do next, in order

1. Collect the parts (G3), including the TS-7's HDMI flat-cable kit, two 90° micro-USB leads and three 90° USB-A adapters, and run `rtl_433 -f 315M` while driving to log the four tyre IDs. Identify your OBD-II dongle (Bluetooth Classic or BLE, PIN, MAC address) and measure its parked current (B8a); it is optional and blocks nothing.
2. Print the carrier's 1:1 assembly drawing, set the sockets and terminals on it, then order the carrier: 5 boards, 2 assembled ([order steps](../01-hardware/v2/carrier/README.md#order-it-at-jlcpcb), G7–G8).
3. Print the template, lay the TS-7 on page 1, and tape a 112 × 124 × 51 mm foam block (the rear box) behind the bezel outline. Mock it up in the car, check the shifter (21.8 mm proud) and choose Option A or B (G5).
4. Measure the TS-7 ([Measure first](../01-hardware/enclosure/README.md#measure-first-then---set)), regenerate the enclosure, and print the bezel, then the housing. Fit the display, the Pi and the carrier (G6), then run B7.
5. Meanwhile, write the software on the bench (the second DevKit and the CC1101 on a breadboard, the Pi with two inventory cameras):
   - the ESP32 application around the tested logic ([02-firmware/v2](../02-firmware/v2/README.md)): the CC1101 driver and EG53MA4 decoder from `bringup/` with carrier-sense gating ([RF-01](../05-Audit-Subsystems/13-tpms-radio.md)), GPS NMEA, `BluetoothSerial` for the dongle (B8b), LEDC PWM, UART2 to the Pi, and a Wi-Fi OTA service mode ([ESP-01](../05-Audit-Subsystems/02-esp32-controller.md));
   - the Pi app: the two-camera capture and detector (V1 sets the model and frame rate), the 1024 × 600 touch UI to the rules D5, V8 and S3 ([DSP-09](../05-Audit-Subsystems/09-display-and-ui.md)), and the UART link with the touch screen's mute counter ([LNK-07](../05-Audit-Subsystems/07-esp32-pi-link.md)).
6. When the boards arrive: hand-solder the ten SMD power parts, then the sockets and terminals; run the bring-up steps, then B1–B6 (G4).
7. After the unit is built: T1 in the car.
